## Meeting Summary
This meeting serves as a high-level overview of the machine learning systems and threat detection strategies utilized by Phil's team, aimed at exploring potential collaboration with an experienced data scientist. Phil explains that his team manages Mail Marshal, threat research, manual malware analysis, and security updates, but he spends the majority of the meeting detailing their three primary machine learning tools developed over the last five to six years.

The Three Core Machine Learning Systems Phil provides a detailed breakdown of the three distinct models currently operating in production:
* URL Deep: This is a deep learning model that analyzes URL strings to predict their "phishiness". It tokenizes the string and feeds it through neural network layers to autogenerate features and output a probability score. The training data primarily consists of phishing URLs extracted from intercepted emails, as well as feeds from sources like Phishtank and the Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG).
* PageML: Operating as a traditional machine learning model, PageML goes a step further by crawling the URL, following redirects, and extracting approximately 120 features from both the downloaded HTML content and the URL string. In addition to its ML capabilities, PageML utilizes a rules engine equipped with Regex and Yara rules to manually mitigate false positives, and it even incorporates the URL Deep model's output into its final scoring. Phil notes that the PageML model is actually overdue for a feature set update, but other priorities have gotten in the way.
* Defense: This traditional machine learning system analyzes the raw structural components of an email, specifically extracting features from the headers and the body (HTML or plain text). Phil emphasizes that Defense targets the underlying structural infrastructure that attackers use to generate and send emails, rather than focusing solely on the ever-changing written content.

Detection Strategy: The Scoring System Approach A crucial insight shared by Phil is that machine learning models are almost never used in isolation to outright block emails. He notes that relying solely on an ML model's prediction inevitably leads to false positives, as the models can only get you so far. Instead, the outputs from models like URL Deep and Defense are translated into a weighted scoring system within Mail Marshal. For example, a highly confident "malicious" prediction from URL Deep might contribute 22 points toward a required conviction threshold of 60 points. This means that other structural rules or threat indicators must also trigger for an email to be definitively classified as phishing.

Current Challenges and the Threat Landscape Phil outlines several operational and threat-based challenges his team faces:
* Data Management Overhead: While the theoretical research part of ML is straightforward to them, the actual day-to-day mechanics of managing and refreshing training data is their biggest bottleneck. Ensuring data is correctly labeled, evaluating what constitutes "good" vs. "bad" data, and building internal testing systems to prove a new model is better than an old one consumes most of their time.
* The Shift to Phishing Links: The threat landscape has shifted significantly; true malicious attachments (like the macro-laden documents or purely malicious PDFs of the past) have drastically decreased over the last decade. Today, the "explosion" is in phishing campaigns utilizing embedded links, sometimes hiding them within otherwise benign-looking PDFs or compromised SharePoint sites.
* Handling Attachments via Rules: While ML could theoretically be applied to attachments, Phil's team currently finds attachments relatively "easy" to manage using traditional rules-based engines. Mail Marshal is highly effective at unpacking nested files (e.g., extracting an RTF document hidden inside a Word document) and scanning them against Yara rules and third-party antivirus layers.

Team Structure and Potential Collaboration The team relies on a few key individuals: a developer named Lloyd serves as the primary ML expert who glues the research and coding together, while Rodell, Carla, and Kevin manage the data collection and model updates for URL Deep, PageML, and Defense, respectively.
Regarding collaboration, Phil recognizes the value of consulting with a research-oriented data scientist to gain new directions and insights. They discuss the possibility of exploring machine learning applications for email attachments as a potential first project. However, Phil cautiously notes that any new project requires careful planning, data provisioning, and regular check-ins, meaning his team must carefully weigh the time investment required to collaborate against their current daily priorities. The meeting concludes with an agreement that they can schedule a future, more in-depth technical session on these systems if desired.
