## Meeting Summary
This meeting serves as a comprehensive strategy and handover session between the Spider Labs Threat Intelligence team (led by Santiago Cortes Diaz, with Jose Manuel Martin Rodriguez and Inigo Lopez-Barranco) and the incoming AI/Data Science team (Inbar Dekel, Aviad Cohen, Itamar, and Guy). Following the departure of a former data science team member, Hessam, the AI team is taking over several orphaned machine learning and AI projects.

Spider Labs Overview and StrategySantiago explains that Spider Labs focuses heavily on threat intelligence, acting as their own customer by developing tools to increase their capabilities and speed. A major goal is to bridge the gap between isolated company departments by providing them with relevant intelligence. They utilize two main platforms for this:

TIPPER: An internal database and pipeline that ingests 50 to 70 new reports daily, used to create faster detections and write rules for internal systems.

Martin News: An external-facing portal that consumes data from TIPR to expose customized, relevant intelligence to other departments, such as managed services, sales, and hunting teams, allowing them to easily address customer needs.
The core of the meeting focuses on three distinct AI/ML projects the Spider Labs team needs assistance with:

Project 1: Open Source Intelligence Chatbot for Martin NewsThe team has an existing chatbot proof-of-concept (POC) built by Hessam to answer analyst questions regarding the open-source threat landscape, such as trending threats, CVEs, or vulnerabilities.
Architecture: It is built on AWS Bedrock Agent Core runtime and uses Anthropic's Haiku model to route requests to appropriate tools.
Data Sources: It utilizes an AWS Bedrock knowledge base (a managed vector store/RAG solution) containing the last three months of news data from TIPR. For older data or data regarding CVEs, APTs, and ransomware, it requires Model Context Protocol (MCP) endpoints connecting to a separate database, which are not yet fully built.
Capabilities: The bot features persistent memory to maintain conversation context across different sessions and "Lambda tools" (static Python functions) to perform actions like downloading content from URLs. The AI team has the freedom to maintain this AWS architecture or migrate it to their own orchestrated agent system. This project is deemed the "low-hanging fruit" and will likely be the first project the AI team tackles.

Project 2: Domain Risk Scoring (Themis)Themis is a mature, production-level machine learning pipeline used to score the risk of domain names, classifying them as benign, gray benign, gray malicious, or malicious.
How it Works: It extracts numerous features and feeds them through a stacking ensemble of multiple ML models (XGBoost, neural networks, random forest). The outputs of these models are then sent as features to a final meta-learner or estimator.
The Challenge: Themis was designed to replace an older, 8-year-old model (Papillion), but the older model is still used in some systems because Themis occasionally flags well-known benign domains (like Google) as malicious.
The Request: Spider Labs needs a way to automate the retraining pipeline for Themis—comparing it against known malicious domains in OTX pulses—so it can be reliably updated every few months and fully replace the legacy model. The AI team noted that building an automated training pipeline for such a complex ensemble is a heavier lift and suggested deferring this to the longer-term future.

Project 3: Infrastructure of Interest (IOI)The IOI pipeline tracks domain names and flags them as suspicious before they are officially deployed in active malware campaigns by monitoring things like early "whois" domain registration records.
Enrichment and ML Weighting: Currently, observations are enriched with about 50 true/false flags derived from systems like Themis and Mather (a TLS certificate binary classifier). Right now, these flags apply static numerical values to a score; if the score passes a threshold of 1.2, the domain is published as a malicious indicator of compromise (IOC) on OTX. Spider Labs wants the AI team to replace these static values with a trained machine learning model that applies proper dynamic weights based on labeled datasets of known benign and malicious domains. Santiago views this weighting model as a high priority.
Campaign Assembler (Clustering): IOI processes up to 20,000 domain observations daily. Jose is currently using older, legacy clustering methods to group these domains into larger "campaigns" using features like Shodan data, DNS records, and customer observations. Spider Labs is requesting a data scientist to analyze this data and implement a more mature, AI-driven clustering solution. This will help the research team prioritize which massive malware campaigns are the most impactful and relevant for PR and deep-dive research.
Resource Allocation and Next StepsThe meeting concludes with a discussion on bandwidth. Santiago requests that the AI team dedicate a consistent portion of their time to internal threat intelligence research to avoid being entirely consumed by platform product managers who are "always in a hurry". Inbar agrees to an approximate 50/50 split between the "Phoenix" platform project and Spider Labs' internal projects, noting that while the ratio may fluctuate based on urgent platform needs, the AI team will strive to keep at least one high-priority internal project actively moving forward.
