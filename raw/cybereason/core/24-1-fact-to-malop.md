|Facts|Facts represent data collected from endpoint<br>sensors that is sent to the Detection servers.<br>Facts include the properties of different<br>activities on the machine on which a sensor is<br>installed, including (but not limited to):<br>Processes<br>Machines<br>Files<br>Connections<br>Services<br>Modules<br>For details on the specific data collected by<br>sensors, see Collected System-Level data<br>(/s/knowledge-base?article=24-1-endpoint-<br>data-collection&language=en_US#collected-<br>system-level-data).<br>The Cross-Machine Correlation (CMC) Engine<br>on the detection server collects facts from all<br>associated machines. The CMC Engine then<br>correlates Facts across all machines and<br>enriches the analysis with artificial intelligence,<br>machine learning, behavioral analysis, and<br>information from threat intelligence sources.<br>The CMC Engine analyzes the collected facts<br>based on Cybereason's threat intelligence, and<br>then generates evidence or suspicions, the next<br>levels of the pyramid, to explain patterns of<br>behavior on the machines in your organization.|
|---|---|
|**Evidence**|**Evidence** describe patterns of behaviors that<br>the Cybereason platform detects on a machine.<br>For example, when a Process Element connects<br>to an RDP port, the CMC Engine generates the<br>**Connected to RDP Port** evidence.<br>Evidence can be benign or malicious.|
|**Suspicions**|Above evidence in the pyramid are**suspicions**.<br>The CMC Engine generates a suspicion when<br>an individual activity is potentially malicious, or<br>when several pieces of evidence, taken<br>together, might represent malicious activity.<br>In general, the threshold for evidence to<br>become a suspicion is deliberately low to<br>minimize the likelihood the Cybereason platform<br>will miss an attack.|
